# ADR-0013: Personal data on the log: classes, what may be published, erasure

**Status:** proposed; the DPO and counsel review it (cards X-09, K-01, TR-05) · **Date:** 2026-10-26 · **Revision:** 0.2 (audit 2026-10-26: bounded compaction lag, who mints references, e-Devlet pairwise ids)

**Context.** Kafka topics are replicated three times and, by design, hard to edit. KVKK sets duties on erasure and destruction (Art. 7), transfer abroad (Art. 9) and data security (Art. 12); counsel confirms the mapping on TR-05. The corridor spec reduces location traces to H3 resolution 8 after 90 days (CP-KPI-004). Receipts and the audit trail in the consent family are kept [10] years unless counsel says otherwise (RC-NFR-002), and that store is the record, not Kafka. Vehicle identity carries VIN and registration data, owner data and payment links, some of which must never be broadcast.

**Options.** (A) Allow personal data on restricted topics and encrypt. (B) Keep personal data out of events entirely and pass references. (C) Allow it with crypto-shredding for erasure.

**Decision.** Option B by default, C as an exception by ADR.
1. **Classes** for every dataset: `public`, `pseudonymous`, `personal`, `restricted` (payment, ownership, consent evidence). Only `public` and `pseudonymous` may appear on a topic.
2. **Pseudonymous is still personal data** under KVKK and GDPR. It lowers exposure; it does not remove the duty to have a basis, a retention rule and an access rule.
3. **Never on Kafka:** VIN, plate, national ID or tax number, phone, e-mail, name, address, card number, IBAN, registration-document images, raw location traces, OBD2 data, free text typed by a person.
4. **References:** `driverRef` and `vehicleRef` are random identifiers minted at registration, `driverRef` by the account service (the OIDC provider's account record, the only place that maps it to a person; open item: confirm the owner on P0-16) and `vehicleRef` by `plugcharge`. They are not derived from a phone number, VIN or plate. Resolving one is an API call under authorization. The invoice recipient is resolved by the recipient resolver at issue time (owner on the session date), never from a plate alone.
5. **Keys** are never direct personal identifiers (VIN, plate, TCKN, VKN, phone, e-mail, name, address, card or account number); the checker rejects them (R3). Pseudonymous refs are personal data too (item 2), so a person-level ref such as `driverRef` is a key only where ADR-0010 item 5 allows it, and is tombstoned on erasure (item 7).
5a. **Externally shared identifiers.** EMAID (`contract-changed`) and the OCPI token uid (`token-changed`) are not minted by us alone: hubs and CPOs hold them too, and can link them to a person. They are classed `pseudonymous` and linkable, so deleting our mapping does **not** make their records unlinkable. They stay as keys of these two compacted topics because hub sync is keyed by them, and erasure for them is the tombstone path in item 7 with a bounded compaction lag, plus token invalidation at hubs and CPOs through `ocpi-emsp` and `hub-gateway`. The DPO signs off this classification and the erasure lag on X-09; if the DPO does not, these topics move to internally minted keys (`contractRef`, `tokenRef`) and carry EMAID or uid only in the hub-bound payload.
5b. **e-Devlet grant ids.** The `upstreamGrantId` is issued by the state Mobility Consent Service, which can link it to the citizen, so it is externally linkable like item 5a. No hub reads `ev.edevlet.upstream-grant-changed.v1` (its consumers are `consent-registry` and `plugcharge`), so nothing needs it as a key: `edevlet-bridge` keys the topic by an internally minted `upstreamGrantRef` and keeps the e-Devlet id in its own store. The DPO signs off this topic separately on X-09.
6. **Retention:** volume 7 d, business facts 30 d, state topics compacted. Longer retention lives in the owner's store with its own lifecycle.
7. **Erasure:** delete the mapping (`driverRef` to person) in the owning service; the log then holds unlinkable data. For compacted state topics also publish a tombstone for every key of that person (each `grantId`, `vehicleRef`, `emaid`, `tokenUid` and `upstreamGrantRef`, and the `driverRef` of `policy-changed`), and set `segment.ms` 1 d, `max.compaction.lag.ms` [1 d] and `delete.retention.ms` 1 d, so the old values are gone in about [3] days whatever the topic's traffic. Without `max.compaction.lag.ms` a quiet topic may never be compacted. Rehearse in the erasure drill.
7a. **Audit is not erased by grant tombstones.** `consent-audit` keeps its own append-only store and ignores tombstones (ADR-0011 item 2). `consent-registry`, which writes grants, has no write or delete path to that store, so no component can both write grants and rewrite the audit trail. Audit records follow their own counsel-approved retention (RC-NFR-002), not the erasure path in item 7.
8. **Location:** positions go to `planner` over HTTP, are stored there with the 90-day reduction, and never reach a topic. Only `arrival-observed` (pseudonymous trip, EVSE) and trip state do.
9a. **e-Devlet references:** the state service gives each recipient a different opaque citizen id (Solution design of the e-Devlet request doc). That id lives only in `edevlet-bridge`, mapped to our `driverRef`; it never goes on a topic, and erasure deletes the mapping.
9. **Logs and traces:** no personal data; trace ids and `correlationid` only.
10. **Transfers:** hub-bound data leaves through `hub-gateway` after the consent decision (RC-GATE-001..004), under the standard-contract basis counsel confirms (TR-05). Kafka itself stays in Türkiye.

**Consequences.**
- Services need an extra call to resolve references; analytics works on references and pseudonymous trips, which is what the KPIs need.
- The schema linter reads a `pii` tag on fields and fails the build. The catalogue checker fails on a non-allowed class or a personal key.
- If a future payload really needs personal data, write an ADR choosing option C: per-subject keys in the vault, with the key deletion as the erasure.
- The platform data inventory (P0-17) records each topic with its class and expiry.
